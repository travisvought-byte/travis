Federal grants

Federal grants run on one rulebook, the Uniform Guidance at 2 CFR 200. Learn its shape once and every agency's notice gets easier to read.

Checked against each source on November 27, 2026. Field practice marks experienced practitioners' judgment rather than a rule or a dataset.

Plan for a month, not two weeks: anything that needs correcting restarts the clock. Field practice Keep the SAM registration active and current the whole time an application is pending or an award is open, and review it at least yearly.

Sources: Grants.gov: organization registration; 2 CFR 25.200, keeping SAM.gov registration active.

Read the funding notice like a reviewer

Every federal Notice of Funding Opportunity has to tell you how you'll be judged. Since the 2024 revision, notices follow a streamlined, plain-language template. Read these parts first, in this order:

  1. Eligibility and responsiveness criteria. These are go/no-go screens. Fail one and nobody scores the rest.
  2. Review criteria. The notice must describe each criterion and sub-criterion, and give weights or points when criteria vary in importance. Your outline should mirror them.
  3. The review and selection process. It must describe merit review and how the agency uses the scores to decide, including any other factors such as geography or past performance.
  4. Submission requirements. Forms, page limits, file formats and the deadline time zone.

Sources: 2 CFR 200 Appendix I, what a funding notice must say; EPA: what's new in the 2024 revision to 2 CFR 200.

How you're judged: two reviews

Merit review: is the project good?

Agencies must run an objective merit review of discretionary applications against written standards, and the process must be described in the notice. Reviewers score your application against the published criteria, not against what they wish you'd proposed.

Source: 2 CFR 200.205, merit review.

Risk review: can you be trusted with the money?

Before an award, the agency reviews the risk you pose. It should consider five things:

  • Financial stability
  • Management systems and standards
  • History of performance on past awards
  • Audit reports and findings
  • Ability to carry out the requirements of the award

For awards above the simplified acquisition threshold, the agency must also review your responsibility and integrity records in SAM.gov. A good score with a weak risk review can still mean no award, or an award with extra conditions.

Source: 2 CFR 200.206, review of risk.

Indirect costs

An organization without a negotiated indirect cost rate may charge up to 15% of modified total direct costs with no documentation to justify it, for as long as it likes. A negotiated rate must be accepted by every federal agency unless a statute or regulation requires otherwise or the agency approves an exception.

Source: 2 CFR 200.414, indirect costs.

Allowable costs

To be charged to a federal award, a cost must be necessary and reasonable for the project and allocable to it, follow the cost rules and the award's limits, be treated consistently with your other activities, not count as both direct and indirect, follow generally accepted accounting principles, not be used as cost share on another federal award, and be adequately documented.

Source: 2 CFR 200.403, allowable costs.

Records

Keep every record for three years from the date you submit the final financial report, longer if an audit, claim or lawsuit starts in that window.

Source: 2 CFR 200.334, record retention.

Errors that sink applications before review

  • Attachment file namesGrants.gov says to use only letters, numbers and a short list of symbols (underscore, hyphen, space, period, parentheses, braces, brackets, ampersand, tilde, exclamation point, comma, semicolon, apostrophe, at sign, number sign, dollar, percent, plus, equals) and to keep names to 50 characters or fewer. Breaking the rule can get the whole application rejected. Safest habit: short names with underscores, like Budget_Narrative.pdf.
  • Roles not assignedIf the person submitting hasn't been given the right role by your E-Business Point of Contact, Grants.gov refuses the submission. Test it days before the deadline.
  • An expired SAM registrationRegistration must be active while the application is under consideration. Check the expiration date before you start writing.
  • Submitting on the last dayValidation errors show up after you press submit. Leave days to fix them. Field practice

Sources: Grants.gov: error messages; 2 CFR 25.200, keeping SAM.gov registration active.

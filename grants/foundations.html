Foundations and companies

A foundation is a small team with its own priorities and a legal duty to give money away. Your job is to show you're the best way to do what it already wants done.

Private foundations have to give

A private foundation must pay out a minimum distributable amount each year, built from a minimum investment return of 5% of its non-charitable-use assets, or pay a 30% excise tax on what it failed to distribute. That money can include its own programs and administrative costs, and it chooses the recipients. So "they have to give" is true; "they have to give to you" is not.

Sources: IRS: private foundation minimum investment return; IRS: private foundation failure to distribute.

Read their tax return before their website

Pull the last three 990-PF returns free on Nonprofit Explorer and look at the list of grants paid. Write down Field practice:

  • Who: what kinds of organizations, and are any like yours?
  • Where: the cities and counties of grantees.
  • How much: the typical grant, not the biggest one.
  • How often: the same names every year means a closed circle; new names every year means an open door.
  • Assets: roughly 5% of them is the floor on what they'll pay out.

How to approach

  • Follow their process exactly. If the guidelines say letter of inquiry first, send a letter of inquiry. If they say by invitation only, don't send a proposal.
  • Call or email the program officer with one specific question the guidelines don't answer. It's how relationships start. Field practice
  • Write a letter of inquiry of one to two pages: who you are, the problem in numbers, what you'll do, what will change, how much you need and what else funds it. Candid's free search at libraries includes a letter of inquiry generator. Candid
  • Report on time, every time. Your next grant from the same funder depends more on the last report than on the next proposal. Field practice

Project money or operating money

Project grants pay for a defined program and must be tracked separately. General operating support pays for the organization as a whole and is the most valuable kind because it covers what projects don't: rent, finance, leadership. Ask for operating support where a funder offers it, and price projects with their real share of overhead where it doesn't. Field practice

Companies

A company foundation is a private foundation under the same payout rule, and its 990-PF is public. Giving straight from the company is a business decision: it follows where the company has employees, customers and a story to tell. Lead with the overlap, offer employee volunteer days and recognition the company can use, and ask whether it matches employee gifts. Field practice

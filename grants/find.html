Where grants are, and how to qualify a funder

Every serious grant is posted somewhere official or shows up in a funder's tax return. Paid databases help, but the free sources below cover most of the field.

Checked against each source on November 27, 2026. Field practice marks experienced practitioners' judgment rather than a rule or a dataset.

Federal

  • Grants.govWhere federal agencies post discretionary funding notices and where most applications are submitted. Searching is free and needs no account.
  • SAM.govRegistration home for any organization taking federal money, and the place to look up assistance listings that describe each federal program.
  • USAspending.govWho actually received past awards from a program, how much and where. Use it to see whether organizations your size and type win, and to find the state agencies that pass money down.

Federal money often reaches small organizations second-hand: a state or county wins the federal award and runs its own competition. Those subawards carry most federal cost rules with them. Field practice

Ohio and other states

Ohio's Office of Budget and Management runs the Ohio Grants Partnership, the central place where state agencies, boards and commissions post funding opportunities. The site is grants.ohio.gov.

  • Search under Funding Opportunities and filter by category, agency, eligible applicant, or federal program number.
  • Get an OH|ID. Applicants need one to apply in the portal. Create it free at ohid.ohio.gov.
  • Set up an Ohio payee ID through Ohio Pays so the state can pay you. It can take several days.
  • Apply in the portal, or follow the opportunity's "More Information" link when the agency takes applications elsewhere. You can save a draft, and before the deadline you can unsubmit, edit and resubmit.

Sources: Ohio Grants Partnership policy (Ohio OBM); Ohio OBM job aid: searching and applying for funding.

The state's job aid is dated 2023; the screens may have changed since.

Any other state

The same pattern works everywhere: look for a central state grants portal (often run by the budget office), then the agencies closest to your work, then the federal pass-through programs those agencies run. Check whether the state needs its own login and vendor or payee registration before you can be paid. Field practice

Foundations

  • Candid search, free at librariesCandid, which absorbed Foundation Directory and GuideStar, is free at more than 2,180 partner locations, mostly public libraries, including saved searches, a letter of inquiry generator and lists of open requests for proposals. Candid also runs hundreds of free courses at learning.candid.org. Candid
  • Foundation tax returns (Form 990-PF)Private foundations list the grants they paid on their 990-PF. Reading two or three years tells you what a foundation really funds, at what size and where, better than its website does. Free on Nonprofit Explorer. ProPublica
  • Community foundationsMost counties are served by one. They run local competitive grants, manage donor-advised funds and usually know which local family foundations fund what. Start with the one covering your county. Field practice

Companies

Company foundations are private foundations and file the same 990-PF, so you can read their giving. Company giving programs run out of the business, favor places where they have employees or customers, and often start with an employee who volunteers with you. Employee matching gifts can double donations you already get. Field practice

Qualify a funder before you write a word

Five questions, in order. One "no" usually means stop.
QuestionWhere the answer lives
Do we fit their focus, in their words?Funder guidelines; the funding notice's eligibility and responsiveness criteria
Do they fund our geography?Guidelines; the addresses of past grantees on the 990-PF or USAspending
Do they fund organizations like us, at our size?Past grant lists: type of grantee, typical award
Do they accept unsolicited requests?Guidelines. "By invitation only" means no proposal; build a relationship or move on.
Is our ask in their usual range?Median past award, not the largest one

Federal funding notices must state eligibility, responsiveness criteria and how applications are reviewed: 2 CFR 200 Appendix I. The rest is Field practice.

Spot a grant scam

The government doesn't award grants for personal needs like bills or debt, doesn't call you about a grant you didn't apply for, doesn't award grants by drawing, and never charges a fee to apply or to receive a federal grant. Anyone asking for a "processing fee", gift cards, a wire or crypto is a scammer. Report it at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.

Sources: Grants.gov: grant-related scams; FTC consumer alert, March 2026; USAGov: avoid free money scams.

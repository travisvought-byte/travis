No 501(c)(3)? Use a fiscal sponsor

A fiscal sponsor is an existing nonprofit that takes in money for your project, so you can start work before, or instead of, forming your own charity.

Checked against each source on November 27, 2026. Field practice marks experienced practitioners' judgment rather than a rule or a dataset.

What fiscal sponsorship is

Fiscal sponsorship isn't defined in law. It describes a relationship between a tax-exempt nonprofit, the sponsor, and a project founder who wants to carry out charitable work. Two setups are common.

The two common models
Comprehensive (Model A)Pre-approved grant (Model C)
What the project isA program inside the sponsor, no different to a regulator from the sponsor's own projectsA separate grantee of the sponsor
Who is in chargeDay-to-day management delegated to the project's director and committee, under the ultimate authority of the sponsor's boardThe project runs itself; the sponsor is the grantmaker, after advance due diligence
Who employs staffUsually the sponsor Field practiceThe project's own entity Field practice
Who the funder paysThe sponsorThe sponsor, which re-grants to the project

What to check before you sign

Field practice

  • A written agreement that names the model, the fee, who owns what the project creates, and how you leave, including taking your money and work with you to your own 501(c)(3).
  • The fee and what it buys: accounting, payroll, insurance, grant reporting.
  • How fast you get paid and who approves spending.
  • Mission fit. The sponsor must be able to say your project furthers its own charitable purpose.
  • Funder acceptance. Ask each funder whether it accepts sponsored projects before you apply. Government funders often require the sponsor to apply as the lead.

Fee ranges vary widely and no public dataset this guide could verify sets a standard; ask several sponsors.

In Ohio

A sponsored project solicits under its sponsor's registration with the Ohio Attorney General; a project that forms its own charity must register within six months. Ohio AG

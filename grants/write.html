Write it the way it will be scored

Reviewers score against a list. Give them the list back, in the funder's order and words, with evidence on every line.

Federal notices must publish their review criteria, with weights when criteria differ in importance (2 CFR 200 Appendix I). Foundations usually publish what they look for in guidelines. The section guidance below is Field practice from how review panels work; the budget rules cite the regulation.

Checked against each source on November 27, 2026. Field practice marks experienced practitioners' judgment rather than a rule or a dataset.

Before you write: the go/no-go read

  • Eligible? Entity type, location, size, registrations.
  • Responsive? Does the project do what this notice is for, not something adjacent?
  • Can you meet every requirement? Cost share, data collection, partners, reporting.
  • Outline from the criteria. One heading per criterion, in their order, using their words.

Need

Show the problem in numbers for the place you serve, from a named public source, and compare it to the state or national rate. Then show the gap: who is already working on it and why that isn't enough. Avoid the need of your organization ("we need a van"); describe the need of the people ("62 residents can't reach appointments"). Every number gets a source and a year.

Approach

Say exactly what you'll do, for whom, how often, by whom and on what timeline. Tie it to evidence that it works: research, a model others have tested, or your own results. A logic model (inputs, activities, outputs, outcomes) on one page helps a reviewer see that the pieces connect.

Outcomes and evaluation

Outputs are what you do (120 visits). Outcomes are what changes (80% of residents keep their housing a year later). Give each outcome a target, a baseline, how you'll measure it, when, and who collects the data. Keep them few and measurable; reviewers distrust ten soft outcomes more than three firm ones.

Organizational capacity

Name the people who will run it and what they've run before. Show your financial controls and past grants managed. This section speaks directly to the federal risk review of financial stability, management systems, performance history and audit findings (2 CFR 200.206).

Budget and justification

The budget is where reviewers check whether the narrative is real. Every person, activity and purchase in the narrative needs a line, and every line needs a sentence explaining it.

  • Match the narrative. Two staff in the text means two salaries in the budget.
  • Allowable and reasonable. Federal costs must be necessary, reasonable, allocable and documented (2 CFR 200.403).
  • Indirect costs. Without a negotiated rate you may use up to 15% of modified total direct costs on federal awards (2 CFR 200.414). Foundations set their own caps; ask.
  • Equipment. Items of $10,000 or more per unit are equipment under the 2024 federal rules, with their own tracking duties (EPA summary).
  • Show the whole picture. Other funding, in-kind support and what this grant covers.

Sustainability

Say what happens when the grant ends: which costs end, which shift to other funders or earned income, and which partners take on what. "We will seek additional grants" is not a plan.

Final pass

  1. Score your own draft against every criterion, out loud, with a colleague who hasn't read it.
  2. Check every number against its source and every budget line against the text.
  3. Check page limits, fonts, file names and required forms.
  4. Submit days early.

A grant writer can help with all of this. Pay them a flat or hourly fee: professional ethics codes bar working for a percentage of the grant (AFP Standard 21; Standards for Excellence).

What the Field practice tag means

Field practice marks guidance drawn from how grantmakers and reviewers commonly work, not from a regulation, a funder's published rule or a dataset. It's labeled so you can weigh it accordingly.

How the guide was planned

It was built as an operational plan:

  • The problem. Small and new organizations lose grants on things that have nothing to do with the quality of their work: status problems, registration delays, misread criteria, budgets that don't match, and advice that's opinion passed off as rule.
  • Information needed. What funders check, what reviewers score, the money rules, where opportunities are posted, who actually gives, and how groups without a 501(c)(3) can take part.
  • Where it lives. Federal regulation (2 CFR 200 and 25), Grants.gov and SAM.gov, IRS rules and data, the Ohio Office of Budget and Management and Attorney General, Giving USA, Candid, foundation tax returns, and professional ethics codes.
  • Gaps. Listed below.

Known gaps

  • State pages beyond Ohio. The pattern on Find grants applies; state-by-state pages are next.
  • Published success rates by federal program and foundation. Few funders publish them; where an agency does, it will be added.
  • Fiscal sponsor fee data from a verifiable survey.
  • A list of Ohio community foundations by county.

Grants for Real is a free guide. It explains rules and public data in plain words; it is not legal or tax advice, and no funder pays to be on it.

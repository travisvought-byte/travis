Where charitable money comes from

Grants are a slice of a bigger picture. In 2025 Americans gave $617.20 billion to charity, the first year over $600 billion. Living individuals gave most of it. Foundations gave about one dollar in five, and companies about one in fourteen.

  • Individuals$394.2 billion, about 64%
  • Foundations$117.15 billion, about 19%
  • Bequests$62.19 billion, about 10%
  • Corporations$43.67 billion, about 7%

What that means for planning: foundation and corporate grants are real money, but an organization that lives only on grants is chasing a quarter of private giving. Grants work best alongside individual donors, earned income and government contracts.

Source: Giving USA 2026 press release.

Percent shares are calculated from Giving USA's dollar figures.

The four kinds of grant money

  • FederalThe biggest single awards and the heaviest rules: registration in SAM.gov, a published funding notice, merit review, risk review, and cost rules in 2 CFR 200.
  • StateOhio posts state agency funding opportunities in one place, grants.ohio.gov. Much state money is federal money passed through, so the federal cost rules often follow it.
  • Private and community foundationsEach sets its own focus, geography and process. Private foundations must pay out a minimum amount every year, and their tax returns show exactly whom they funded.
  • CorporateCompany foundations follow the private foundation rules; company giving programs and employee matching follow the company's own.
